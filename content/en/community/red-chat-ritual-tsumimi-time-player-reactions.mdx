## What Players Praise: The Stream-Intervention Fantasy

The strongest positive reactions cluster around one idea: nobody has framed a horror game quite this way before. Instead of controlling a protagonist with a flashlight, you control a wallet and a keyboard. Community reports repeatedly highlight how uncomfortable it feels to realize your "harmless" chat message moved the plot — which is precisely the emotional response a psychological horror game wants.

Player experience also favors the structure. The day/night rhythm gives investigation a purpose: every search result, fan-site post, and reply thread feeds the next broadcast. The Investigation Files system lets you revisit collected clues, so the detective work feels tangible rather than vague. Multiple endings, tracked through achievements like End 6, End 7, and End 8, reward players who replay with different intervention strategies.

## What Players Criticize: Pacing, Economy, and Deduction Logic

Negative reactions tend to center on friction rather than concept. The most common theme in community reports is the pace of the fifteen-day loop: players who want momentum can feel stuck re-reading threads and re-checking locations while the clock ticks. A second recurring theme is the money economy — Mega Chats and gifts cost limited funds, so a single impulsive donation can quietly narrow your options for the rest of a run.

The developer has clearly heard this. A Steam news post dated October 24, 2026 announced fast-forwarding improvements, increased starting funds, and an official Hints & FAQ. That is a direct response to the two loudest complaints: replay speed and early-run budgeting. If you bounced off the launch build, the current build is measurably more forgiving.
