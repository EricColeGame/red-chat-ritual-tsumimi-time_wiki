## What This Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME Walkthrough Covers

The Red Chat Ritual is a chat-driven event: progress depends on what you type, when you type it, and what state the chat channel is in when you do. ### Why players get stuck

In community reports, the same three blockers come up again and again: someone types ahead of the sequence, someone leaves the channel mid-ritual, or the group restarts without clearing the previous attempt. None of these are skill issues. They are coordination issues, and coordination is the part a walkthrough can actually fix.

### The three-minute pre-flight routine

Player experience suggests a short routine saves far more time than it costs. Run it every attempt:

1. Confirm everyone is in the same channel and can see each other's messages.
2. Agree on who speaks first — one caller, not four.
3. Clear the chat with a neutral line so the ritual's first message is visually distinct.
4. Start a screenshot or recording if your setup allows it.
5. Read the first two steps aloud before anyone types anything.

## The Walkthrough, Stage by Stage

The ritual is best understood as a conversation with a fixed structure rather than a list of magic words. This Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME walkthrough breaks it into stages so your group always knows which part of the conversation it is in.

| Stage | Your job | Signal that it worked |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Establish the ritual in chat | Chat responds or the prompt changes |
| Confirmation | Acknowledge the prompt without adding extra lines | The response stays consistent |
| Resolution | Complete the final exchange in order | The event state advances or concludes |
| Cleanup | Exit the channel and confirm the result | No lingering prompt or stuck state |

### Opening the ritual

The opening is where most attempts die. The goal is not to type fast — it is to make your first message unmistakable. If two players send the opening line at once, the chat can register a duplicate and the ritual may treat the attempt as already answered.

Send one opening line, from one player, and wait. Do not fill the silence with "did it work?" messages; those are chat noise and can be read as part of the sequence.

### Holding the confirmation

Once the chat acknowledges you, resist the urge to elaborate. Community reports consistently describe extra messages as the number one cause of a ritual that "half works." If the prompt asks for a short response, give a short response. If it asks for nothing, give nothing.

### Resolving and exiting

The final stage is the most anticlimactic and the most commonly rushed. Confirm the outcome in chat, then leave the channel cleanly. Do not immediately re-enter to "check" — a fast re-entry can register as a new attempt and wipe the confirmation you just earned.

## Timing, Order, and Chat Hygiene

Timing is the least documented part of any Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME walkthrough, largely because exact windows vary by build and connection. What does not vary is the principle: the ritual expects one message per beat, and it expects beats in order.

| Situation | Recommended action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chat is scrolling fast | Pause and let it settle before replying | Replies can land out of order |
| Multiple players typing | Nominate a single caller | Duplicate lines break the sequence |
| You sent the wrong line | Stop, reset, restart cleanly | Correcting mid-sequence rarely works |
| The chat goes silent | Wait one full cycle before reacting | Silence is often a stage, not a failure |
| Someone disconnects | Abort and regroup | Partial parties produce partial progress |

### Order beats speed

A slow, correct run beats a fast, broken one every time. If your group is unsure whether a step landed, the safest move is to stop and restart rather than guess. Guessing produces the worst outcome: a run that looks finished but never resolves.

### Keep a clean chat log

Screenshots are your debugging tool. When a run fails, compare the last few messages against a successful attempt. In player experience, the difference is usually one extra line or one missing acknowledgment — small enough to miss in real time, obvious in a screenshot.

## Troubleshooting: When the Ritual Will Not Advance

If your attempt stalls, work through this table before you retry. Restarting blindly tends to reproduce the same failure.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ritual never starts | Opening line duplicated or mistyped | Clear chat, restart with one sender |
| Prompt appears then vanishes | Extra messages sent during confirmation | Re-run with stricter chat discipline |
| Progress stalls mid-sequence | A player left or joined the channel | Lock the group and repeat from stage one |
| Ritual completes but nothing changes | Result not confirmed before exit | Confirm in chat, then leave cleanly |
| Same failure twice in a row | Stale state from a previous attempt | Fully reset the event before retrying |

### When to stop and ask

If three clean attempts fail in identical ways, the problem is probably not your execution. Check whether your build matches the one the community is currently documenting, and ask in a community thread whether the trigger has changed. A five-minute question is cheaper than a fifth attempt.

## Community Tips and Common Myths

Player experience has produced a lot of folklore around this event. Some of it helps; some of it actively wastes time. Here is how to separate the two.

| Claim | Verdict | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Type faster to beat the timer" | Misleading | Match the chat's rhythm, not a stopwatch |
| "More players make it easier" | Usually false | A small, coordinated group is more reliable |
| "You can fix a broken run mid-sequence" | Rarely true | Reset and start over |
| "The wiki has the exact lines" | Sometimes | Verify, then test with one attempt |
| "Failure means you missed a step" | Often true | Compare screenshots against a clean run |

### Build a small ritual crew

The single biggest quality-of-life improvement reported by players is running the ritual with the same two or three people each time. Familiarity removes the coordination tax: everyone knows who calls, who stays silent, and when to stop. Pick your crew, run it twice, and keep the screenshots.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME walkthrough?**
Not in a single official document, as far as the community has found. Most guidance comes from the community wiki and player experience, which is why verifying details against a recent source matters more here than in most events.

**How many players do I need for the ritual?**
Community reports describe successful runs with small groups, and coordination appears to matter more than headcount. A tight group of two or three usually outperforms a large, chatty party.

**Why does the ritual work for other people but not for me?**
The usual culprits are duplicate opening lines, extra messages during confirmation, or a stale state left over from a previous attempt. Reset fully, nominate one caller, and try again with a clean chat log.

**Can I retry immediately after a failure?**
You can, but a full reset first is strongly recommended. A quick retry often carries the broken state forward, which makes the second failure look like a new problem when it is the same one.
