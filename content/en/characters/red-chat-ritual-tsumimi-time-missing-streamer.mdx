## Meet the Cast of Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME

### Tsumimi: The Idol Who Streams So She'll Be Found

Tsumimi belongs to Wataame Chainsaw, and the game frames her as struggling — not yet famous, not yet safe. Her reason for streaming is almost painfully human: if she gets big enough, Mashiro might notice her and come back. That single motivation reframes every donation you send. You aren't tipping a performer; you're funding a search party that happens to look like a variety show.

Watch how she talks about the village. The streams take place in an abandoned settlement erased from maps, and the announcement hints that its dark history and the incidents that once occurred there surface as your investigation connects scattered fragments. Tsumimi may know more than she lets on, or she may be as lost as you are. The material deliberately leaves that ambiguous, which is exactly why she works as a character rather than a quest-giver.

### Mashiro: The Partner Who Exists as an Absence

Mashiro is Tsumimi's partner who suddenly disappeared, and that is nearly all the announcement gives us. No portrait, no profile, no confirmed whereabouts — just a name that keeps surfacing and a hole in the story shaped precisely like her. In character-design terms, that's deliberate. A missing person is the only kind of character who can be everywhere in a narrative without ever appearing on screen.

That absence is also why the Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME missing streamer question stays interesting across multiple playthroughs. You aren't learning who Mashiro is so much as learning what Tsumimi believes about her — and those two things may not match.

Push hard and you may extract information; push harder and you may steer her toward a fate you didn't intend. Sometimes the gift you send late at night returns as an unexpected reaction in the next day's broadcast, which means even generosity has consequences.

## The Two-Week Cycle: How Night Streams Rewrite the Next Day

The story unfolds over roughly two weeks of day-and-night cycles, and the structure is doing narrative work, not just pacing. Nights are for intervention. Days are for processing: organizing fragments, testing hypotheses, and deciding what to try next. Because the loop is short, a bad call on night three can still be partially repaired by night nine — but never fully erased.

| Phase | In-game timing | What you do | What carries forward |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night | During Tsumimi's broadcast | Send Mega Chats, comments, and gifts | Gifts can trigger new reactions the following day |
| Day | Between streams | Review the investigation file and connect fragments | Confirmed hypotheses unlock new questions at night |
| Final choice | End of the two-week cycle | Commit to a deduction | Determines which ending you reach |

The delayed-gift rule is the quiet genius of the design. It prevents the livestream from becoming a vending machine where input equals output. You spend a night wondering whether that plush toy or that pointed question actually landed, and the answer arrives as a change in Tsumimi's demeanor the next evening. Community reports are still thin on the ground — with no release date announced, there's no player experience to cross-check yet — so treat any exhaustive ending list you find online as speculation rather than fact.

## Investigation and Deduction: Turning Chat Logs Into Endings

Everything you gather lands in an investigation file. Organizing fragments and confirming hypotheses isn't busywork: the game states that it changes what happens next and the ending itself. Your leads are narrow by design — a village erased from the map and the faint irregularities caught on stream — so the file is less a database and more a stack of contradictions you're trying to reconcile.

| Evidence type | Where it comes from | How it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Faint irregularities | Night broadcasts | Flagged and stored in the investigation file |
| Village history | Off-stream investigation | Reconstructs the settlement's erased past |
| Contradictions | Cross-referencing nights | Confirms or breaks your working hypotheses |
| Your own chat log | Your interventions | Shapes how much Tsumimi trusts you |

A few habits make the deduction loop far less frustrating:

- **Treat each stream as a test, not a performance.** Decide what you're trying to learn before you type anything.
- **Log recurring irregularities.** Small visual or verbal glitches are the breadcrumbs; a note on your second monitor beats memory.
- **Alternate your approach across runs.** One generous playthrough and one restrained playthrough will surface different information.
- **Don't confirm a hypothesis just because it's your only one.** The file rewards organized reasoning, not confident guessing.
- **Save before the final choice.** With multiple endings and branching paths, the last decision is the one worth revisiting.

| Ending factor | What it controls |
|---|---|
| Intervention intensity | How hard you push Tsumimi and how she responds |
| Investigation coverage | Which fragments you collected before the deadline |
| Deduction accuracy | Whether your confirmed hypotheses actually hold |
| Final choice | The last commitment you make at the close of the cycle |
