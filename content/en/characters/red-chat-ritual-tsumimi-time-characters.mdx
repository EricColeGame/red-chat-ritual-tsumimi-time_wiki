## Your Character: The Viewer Behind the Keyboard

You are not a silent protagonist. The Steam page describes intervening in the stream through comments and Mega Chats, then using your deductions to change the streamer's fate. That makes your viewer persona a genuine member of the cast — arguably the most important one, since the plot moves when you type.

However, player experience from the demo suggests the game does not always make your own character easy to see. Feedback posted in the [Steam community discussion board for the demo](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4413070/discussions/1/567037607299532829/) points out that the player's messages lack a fixed icon and instead arrive with randomized colored icons.

| Demo issue reported | Player experience | Why it matters for character identity |
|---|---|---|
| No fixed avatar for the player | Your own messages are hard to spot in a busy chat | You lose visual anchoring as a "character" in the scene |
| Randomized icon colors | The same speaker can look like different people | Reads as inconsistency rather than mystery |
| Weak self-recognition | Following your own thread takes extra effort | Breaks immersion during deduction-heavy moments |

Community reports frame this as a readability problem rather than a story problem, and it may well be adjusted. Until then, a few habits help:

- **Pick a handle you can scan instantly.** A short, distinct name beats a long one when the chat scrolls fast.
- **Track your handle, not your icon.** Search the transcript for your name rather than your avatar color.
- **Keep a running log of key exchanges.** Note the day, who spoke, and what was promised.
- **Stay in character.** If you decide your viewer is skeptical, stay skeptical — consistency makes your persona legible even when the UI does not.
