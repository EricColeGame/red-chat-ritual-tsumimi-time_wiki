## The Core Loop: Investigate by Day, Intervene by Night

Every one of the 15 days splits into two halves, and both halves feed each other. At night you are a viewer: you watch Tsumimi's stream, drop comments, fire off Mega Chats, and send gifts. That is your only lever on the story in real time. By day you are an investigator: you take the keywords, names, and odd phrases that surfaced during the stream and run them through search, replies, and fan sites.

Neither half works alone. The stream generates raw material. The daytime investigation turns that raw material into something you can actually reason with. Skipping the search phase because a stream looked uneventful is the single most common way players stall out in the **Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME clue system**.

| Phase | Your Role | Primary Actions | What It Produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night (stream) | Viewer | Comments, Mega Chats, gifts | New keywords, tone shifts, reactions, flags |
| Day (investigation) | Researcher | Keyword searches, reply chains, location details, fan sites | Clue fragments and context |
| Review | Deduction | Reading Investigation Files, linking fragments | Conclusions and unlocked branches |
| Repeat | — | Carried forward across all 15 days | Story divergence and ending routes |

### Why the order matters

A keyword only becomes useful after you resolve it. If you collect a name during a stream and never search it, it sits in your log as dead weight. The game rewards players who treat each night as a data-gathering session and each day as a cleanup pass — resolve what you found, then look for the contradictions.

## Where Clues Actually Hide: Six Surfaces Worth Checking

The store page is unusually direct about this: you are told to check replies to posts and location details, not just headline search results. That is a hint about density. Clues are spread across several surfaces, and most players only check two of them.

| Surface | What You'll Usually Find | Why Players Miss It |
|---|---|---|
| Stream comments | Rumors, hints, audience speculation | Scrolling too fast during live chat |
| Replies to posts | Corrections and follow-up details | Only reading the original post |
| Search results | Unsettling records and background | Stopping at page one |
| Location details | Geographic and timeline anchors | Treating them as flavor text |
| Fan sites | Someone's obsession and archived material | Assuming they're decorative |
| Reactions to gifts/Mega Chats | Immediate tone changes from Tsumimi | Not tracking which message caused what |

### Practical search habits

- **Search the boring words too.** Ordinary nouns and place names are often the load-bearing clues, not the dramatic ones.
- **Open replies before you move on.** A single correction in a reply chain can reframe an entire night's stream.
- **Read location details as evidence.** Where something happened is frequently as important as what happened.
- **Treat fan sites as primary sources.** Obsession leaves records, and records leave contradictions.
- **Note reactions, not just answers.** When Tsumimi's demeanor shifts after a specific comment or gift, that shift is data.

Community reports consistently describe the same trap: players who speed through the daytime phase hit a wall around the mid-game and assume the game is unfair, when in fact they simply never opened the second layer of a post.

## Reading Investigation Files Without Getting Lost

Investigation Files are your collected-information ledger. Everything you gather can be reviewed there, which means the file is not a checklist — it's a workspace. The mistake is treating it as an archive you consult only when stuck.

The better habit is to open the file at the start of each day and ask one question: *what here disagrees with something else?* Contradictions are the engine of the **Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME clue system**. Two sources that cannot both be true are worth more than ten that agree.

| Step | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Skim the file for unresolved entries | A short list of loose ends |
| 2 | Cross-reference entries that mention the same person or place | Candidate contradictions |
| 3 | Take the contradiction back to the stream as a question | A targeted comment or Mega Chat |
| 4 | Observe Tsumimi's response | Confirmation, deflection, or new keyword |
| 5 | Commit to a deduction | Story branch movement |

### Save before you commit

Saving is available from the top of the home screen, and the game's own guidance recommends saving before entering a stream so you can test different choices. Do that. Deductions are the point where a run forks, and a save taken before a stream is the cheapest insurance you have against a bad call.

Money is the constraint that makes the **Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME clue system** a resource puzzle rather than a pure reading exercise. Comments are cheap; Mega Chats and gifts are not. You cannot ask everything, so prioritization is the skill.

## FAQ

**How does the Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME clue system actually work?**
It runs on a two-phase loop. Night streams generate keywords and reactions through comments, Mega Chats, and gifts. Daytime investigation resolves those keywords through search results, replies, location details, and fan sites. You then link fragments in your Investigation Files and commit to a deduction, which moves the story toward one of several endings.

**Do I need to search every keyword I collect?**
Not every one, but you should resolve every keyword tied to a contradiction or a named person and place. Low-value rumors can be skipped when funds and time are tight. Prioritize anything that conflicts with an existing entry in your file.

**Can I change my mind after making a deduction?**
Yes, if you saved beforehand. The game lets you save and load from the home screen, and saving before entering a stream is the recommended habit. Without a save, a committed deduction can lock you into a branch until your next playthrough.

**Is the clue system easier after the recent update?**
It is more forgiving. The developers added fast-forwarding (hold Ctrl during a stream) and increased starting funds, plus an official Hints & FAQ in the Steam Community guides section. The underlying deduction logic is unchanged, but you have more room to experiment before the budget runs out.
