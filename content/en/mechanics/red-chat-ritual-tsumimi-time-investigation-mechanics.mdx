## The Core Loop: Investigate by Day, Intervene by Night

Red Chat Ritual – TSUMIMI TIME, developed and published by Tohakusha Inc., is a psychological horror adventure built on a single structural idea: **investigate by day, intervene by night**. Mashiro, the streamer you follow, has disappeared. Tsumimi is still broadcasting, and her stream is the only thread left to pull.

The game runs across 15 in-game days. Each day gives you a research window, and each night gives you a live broadcast where your input matters. The two halves feed each other — a keyword you look up in the afternoon can unlock a Mega Chat option after dark, and a line Tsumimi drops on stream can point you toward a search term you never tried.

| Phase | What you actually do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day (research) | Search keywords, read post replies, check location details, browse fan sites and unsettling search results | Fills your clue pool before the next broadcast |
| Night (stream) | Watch Tsumimi live; post comments, send Mega Chats, spend on gifts | Shifts the story, unlocks new leads |
| Between phases | Review the Investigation Files, save, plan your budget | Prevents dead ends and wasted funds |

The loop sounds simple, but the pressure comes from scarcity. You have limited money, a fixed number of days, and no way to un-send a message. Community reports describe the pacing as deliberately uncomfortable: you are always one clue short of certainty when the stream starts.

## Keyword Searching and Investigation Files: How Clue Gathering Works

The research half of Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME is essentially a search-engine mystery. You type keywords, read what comes back, and then — this is the part players skip — you open the results properly. The developer's own play guide stresses checking **replies to posts** and **location details**, not just the headline text of a result.

Everything you collect is stored in your Investigation Files, which is your review screen before a broadcast. Treat it like a case board: if a clue is not in the Files, it is not part of your deduction yet.

| Clue source | What it tends to give you | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyword searches | Names, dates, and topic threads | Stopping at the first result page |
| Post replies | Contradictions and personal testimony | Reading only the original post |
| Location details | Confirms whether a story lines up | Ignoring addresses and place names |
| Fan sites | Obsessive records and timeline gaps | Dismissing them as flavor text |
| Stream comments | Rumors that become searchable keywords | Muting the chat entirely |

A practical rhythm that works well: search broad, then narrow. Start with a name or an event, note any new proper nouns, and re-search those. After each stream, run one new keyword you picked up from the broadcast. Repeat the same search later in the game too — the investigation mechanics reward revisiting old terms once you know more.

Also worth knowing: the official play guide and a Hints & FAQ are published through Steam's news and community Guides sections. The FAQ's later half contains walkthrough spoilers, so read the top portion first if you only want a nudge.

## Stream Intervention Mechanics: Comments, Mega Chats, and Gifts

Night is where the game's title earns itself. As a viewer, you intervene in Tsumimi's livestream through three channels — comments, Mega Chats, and gifts — and each has a different cost and a different kind of leverage.

| Tool | Cost | Visibility | Best used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comments | Free | Low — easy to bury in chat | Probing a topic, testing reactions, nudging tone |
| Mega Chats | Paid, high cost | High — Tsumimi reacts directly | Forcing a subject, unlocking branches, pushing a decision |
| Gifts | Paid, variable | Very high | Building rapport, changing mood before a hard question |
| Silence | Free | None | Avoiding a flag, preserving funds for a later night |

The underrated option is saying nothing. The game explicitly asks when you will stay silent, and silence is a legitimate tactic when you are not yet sure which interpretation of the clues is correct. A premature Mega Chat can lock you into a route you did not have the evidence to justify.

Because funds are finite, budget like a player, not a fan. Pick two or three nights where a Mega Chat is genuinely load-bearing, and spend comments everywhere else. Player experience suggests that spreading money evenly across all 15 days leaves you unable to afford the one intervention that mattered.

## Time, Money, and Save Management Across 15 Days

Most ruined playthroughs are not ruined by bad deduction — they are ruined by bad bookkeeping. The game gives you two resources, time and money, and a save system that lets you experiment if you use it deliberately.

| Habit | How to do it | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Save before every stream | Use the save option at the top of the home screen | Lets you test a Mega Chat without committing |
| Keep a "clean" save per day | One file at the start of each day | Cheap insurance against a route collapse |
| Fast-forward replays | Hold **Ctrl** during a stream to jump to the next Mega Chat choice | Skips dialogue you have already read |
| Re-check the Files | Open Investigation Files before each night | Catches clues you collected but never applied |

Two quality-of-life details are worth flagging. First, the developer added fast-forwarding and increased starting funds in a post-launch update, alongside an official Hints & FAQ — so early complaints about pacing and budget have been partially addressed. Second, saving before you enter a stream is the single most recommended tip in the community, because it converts a permanent choice into a testable one.

On hardware, the game is intentionally light: Windows 10 64-bit, 4 GB of RAM, and 4 GB of storage are enough, with integrated graphics listed in the minimum specs. Headphones are strongly recommended for the audio design, and the mature content description warns of psychological horror, violence and blood, and references to self-harm and suicide.

## Deduction and Endings: How Your Choices Reshape the Story

The deduction layer is where Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME stops being a chat simulator. Clues do not resolve automatically; you have to connect fragments and commit to an interpretation. The game's store page is direct about the consequence: your interventions, investigations, deductions, and choices shape Tsumimi's stream and determine how the story ends.

Multiple endings are confirmed, and the Steam achievement list is a useful hint at scale — it includes entries labeled End 6, End 7, and End 8, which implies at least eight distinct conclusions to hunt down. Achievement names like "Long-time stream viewer" suggest that some outcomes reward consistency rather than cleverness, so a single "correct" playstyle is unlikely to be optimal.

| Deduction pitfall | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committing too early | Spending a Mega Chat on a theory with one supporting clue | Wait for a second corroborating source |
| Ignoring tone | Treating hostile comments as neutral questions | Match your message to what you want Tsumimi to feel |
| Over-spending | Empty wallet by day 9 | Cap nightly spending and bank for late-game nights |
| Never revisiting clues | Using each keyword exactly once | Re-run old searches after every major reveal |

## FAQ

**How do the Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME investigation mechanics actually connect day and night?**
Day-phase research fills your Investigation Files, and night-phase comments, Mega Chats, and gifts apply that knowledge to Tsumimi's stream. A clue found in the afternoon typically unlocks a new intervention option after dark, so skipping research narrows your choices.

**Do comments matter, or only Mega Chats?**
Both matter. Comments are free and can probe topics or set a tone, while Mega Chats are expensive but force a direct reaction from Tsumimi. Silence is also a valid third option when your evidence is incomplete.

**How many endings does the game have?**
Multiple endings are confirmed, and Steam achievements reference End 6, End 7, and End 8, implying at least eight. Reaching them requires different intervention and deduction choices, not just a single optimized route.

**What is the best way to avoid a dead end?**
Save at the home screen before entering a stream, keep one clean save per day, and review your Investigation Files before every broadcast. On replays, hold Ctrl to fast-forward to the next Mega Chat choice and test alternatives quickly.
