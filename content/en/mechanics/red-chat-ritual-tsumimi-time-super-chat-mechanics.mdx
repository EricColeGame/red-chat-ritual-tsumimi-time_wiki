## What the Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME Super Chat Mechanics Actually Control

The game's premise is simple on paper: your favorite streamer, Mashiro, has vanished, and the only person with a live channel is Tsumimi. You intervene in her broadcasts through comments, Mega Chats, and gifts, then use what you learn to change the outcome.

"Mega Chat" is the game's own term for what most viewers would call a super chat — a paid, high-visibility message that lands in the stream instead of scrolling past. That naming matters, because the three inputs are not interchangeable. The game does not publish a price list for individual Mega Chats, so treat the table below as a decision framework rather than a fixed tariff.

| Input | Cost | Where it lands | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comment | Free | Standard chat scroll | Testing reactions, fishing for information, staying present without spending |
| Mega Chat | Draws on limited funds | Highlighted message Tsumimi responds to directly | Moments where a plain comment would be ignored or would lock you out |
| Gift | Draws on limited funds | Support sent to the streamer | Shifting the temperature of a stream without committing to a statement |
| Silence | Free | Nothing | Avoiding a reaction you cannot afford to trigger yet |

The critical takeaway: comments are your free reconnaissance, and Mega Chats are your paid steering wheel. Players who treat every night as a spending opportunity usually run dry right when the story starts asking harder questions.

## The Night Loop: How a Stream Session Plays Out

Every night follows a similar rhythm, and understanding it turns a passive watch-along into a repeatable routine.

| Step | What you do | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Save from the top of the home screen | Do this before entering any stream — it is your undo button |
| 2 | Open the night's broadcast | Note the stream title and any recurring chat regulars |
| 3 | Read the chat before typing | Rumors, contradictions, and hints usually surface in viewer messages first |
| 4 | Choose comment, Mega Chat, gift, or silence | Check your remaining funds before you commit |
| 5 | Watch Tsumimi's response in full | Her reaction often carries more information than the message you sent |
| 6 | Log the clue after the stream ends | Anything new should end up in your Investigation Files |

### Use Ctrl to jump to the next Mega Chat decision

Holding the Ctrl key during a stream fast-forwards to the next Mega Chat choice. This is the single most useful quality-of-life feature in the game, especially on a second or third run, because it skips dialogue you have already read without skipping the decisions themselves. It will not carry you past a branch — it stops where the game wants your input.

### Save before you enter a stream, every single time

The save and load options sit at the top of the home screen. Saving immediately before a broadcast means a bad Mega Chat costs you one stream, not one evening. This is the cheapest insurance policy in the game and the habit most new players skip.

## Budgeting Your Limited Funds Across 15 Days

Funds are finite, the calendar is 15 days long, and there are multiple endings. That combination makes spending the real difficulty curve of the game — not the horror.

| Playstyle | Spending pattern | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front-loaded | Heavy Mega Chat use in the first nights | Fast information, early branch control | Nothing left when late reveals demand a paid response |
| Saver | Comments only until a hard gate appears | Large reserve for the finale | Misses early flags that quietly shape later scenes |
| Paced | One or two paid messages per stream, logged | Balanced, easy to audit | Requires actual note-taking |

A few practical rules that hold up across all three styles:

- **Keep a reserve.** Assume the last few nights are the most expensive and never spend to zero.
- **Spend to unlock information, not affection.** A Mega Chat that produces a new clue beats one that produces a nice reaction.
- **Re-read before you re-send.** If a comment gets a flat response, a Mega Chat on the same topic is often wasted money.
- **Note that the balance has shifted.** A post-launch update increased starting funds, so launch-day advice about scraping by on day one is now stricter than the current build.

## Day Phase: Turning Clues Into Deductions

By day you investigate; by night you intervene. The day phase is where your spending pays off, because a Mega Chat that surfaced a name is worthless if you never search it.

| Clue source | Where to look | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Search keywords | The in-game search | Surfaces unsettling records and name matches tied to Mashiro |
| Replies to posts | Threads and comment chains | Corrections and timestamps expose contradictions |
| Location details | Posts and listings | Confirms whether a story physically lines up |
| Fan sites | Community pages | Someone's obsession often leaves a trail |
| Investigation Files | Your menu | Shows gaps in your own timeline before they become dead ends |

The most common failure here is tunnel vision. A single keyword that returns nothing does not mean the lead is dead — it means the phrasing is wrong. Try the name, then the event, then the location. The Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME super chat mechanics feed this loop directly: every paid message you send should generate a searchable term, or it was decoration.

## FAQ

**Are Mega Chats the same thing as super chats?**

Functionally, yes. A Mega Chat is Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME's version of a super chat: a paid, highlighted message that Tsumimi reacts to directly, unlike a free comment that can be ignored. When people search for Red Chat Ritual TSUMIMI TIME super chat mechanics, Mega Chats are what they are looking for.

**Can I undo a bad Mega Chat?**

Yes, if you saved first. Load your pre-stream save from the top of the home screen and replay the night. Without that save, the funds are gone and the branch is set.

**Do I need to spend money every night?**

No. Free comments are a legitimate strategy and often the smarter one, since they let you test Tsumimi's mood before committing funds. The pacing that works best for most players is a small number of deliberate paid messages rather than constant spending.

**How many endings are there?**

The game confirms multiple endings shaped by your interventions, investigations, and deductions, but it does not advertise an exact count. Track your runs in the Investigation Files and treat the ending achievements as your checklist.

**Is the game finished or still being patched?**

It is being actively supported. The developers have already added fast-forwarding, increased starting funds, and an official Hints & FAQ, so check the store page news feed before assuming an older guide still applies.
